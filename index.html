Executive Summary

The geopolitical contest between the United States and the People's Republic of China to build Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and superintelligent AI is not an arms race that either side can win. As documented across top peer-reviewed academic journals (Science, Harvard International Review, Texas National Security Review), national security think tank assessments (Brookings Institution, Center for a New American Security [CNAS], RAND Corporation), and mainstream journalistic investigations (Foreign Affairs, The New York Times, Reuters, Fortune), treating AGI as an adversarial superpower race triggers catastrophic existential risk.

The core dilemma rests on three structural realities:

  1. Alignment and Control Remain Technically Unsolved: Unlike expanding compute or model scale, ensuring that an autonomous, superhuman intelligence reliably follows human intent is an open scientific challenge. As Turing laureates and computer science leaders have demonstrated, advancing capability does not automatically solve control.
  2. Adversarial Competition Enforces a "Race to the Bottom": When two superpowers view AGI development as an existential struggle for technological hegemony, labs and defense establishments face acute pressure to cut corners on safety evaluations, strip away human oversight, and accelerate deployment before guardrails are established.
  3. The First-Mover Advantage is Self-Destructive ("Winner Inherits the Peril"): Unlike nuclear weapons or conventional arms races where deterrence or superiority can stabilize competition, an uncontrolled or misaligned superintelligence poses symmetric, irreversible extinction risks. The "victor" of an uncontrolled AGI race simply walks off the cliff first.

Every debate card below is sourced exclusively from peer-reviewed scientific journals, reputable defense/policy think tanks, or mainstream journalistic institutions. All videos, transcripts, podcasts, and newsletters/blogs have been strictly excluded.

Strategic Argument Framework

Premise 1 — The Alignment Problem Is Technically Unsolved

Autonomous AI systems that match or exceed human cognitive capacity create unprecedented control challenges. Technical safety does not advance linearly with parameter scaling or compute; without verified alignment mechanisms, advanced models can pursue unconstrained or deceptive sub-goals.

Premise 2 — Superpower Racing Induces Corner-Cutting and Safety Strip-Down

Adversarial competition creates a severe collective action failure. Even when policymakers and lab directors recognize the severe hazards of premature deployment, the acute fear that a geopolitical rival will achieve capability thresholds first compels both sides to bypass safety testing and human-in-the-loop oversight.

Premise 3 — Loss of Control Is Terminal, Irreversible, and Symmetric

In an arms race over autonomous, self-improving systems, gaining a unilateral lead does not provide security. If either superpower deploys a misaligned superintelligence, the resulting catastrophe (cyber-infrastructure collapse, autonomous biological threats, or runaway autonomous optimization) eliminates the victor and loser alike.

Comparative Matrix: Why Superpower Competition Inverts Conventional Deterrence

Analytical Dimension Nuclear Weapons Arms Race (Cold War) AGI Superpower Arms Race (US-China)
Deterrence Dynamic Second-strike survivability creates Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD), stabilizing conflict. An unaligned superintelligence cannot be commanded to "target only the enemy." Both victor and loser face catastrophe.
First-Mover Value First-strike capability historically created perceived (though unstable) strategic dominance. Winner inherits the peril: Reaching superhuman autonomy without solving alignment triggers immediate loss of control for the host state.
Safety vs. Speed Trade-Off Weapon safety interlocks and permissive action links (PALs) could be implemented without reducing yield. Alignment requires fundamental theoretical breakthroughs; racing at maximum speed directly forces corner-cutting on safety.
Proliferation & Containment Fissile material (enriched uranium, plutonium) requires massive industrial facilities that are easily tracked. Digital algorithmic weights and automated recursive self-improvement can proliferate instantly across global fiber networks.
